# The `beancount-ask` and `beancount-close` spending recipes sum `cost(position)`, which leaves `@`-priced foreign charges in their own currency, so "USD answers" come out as `300 EUR 1500 JPY` and the anomaly filter compares raw numbers across currencies

**Minor · skills · 20–30m · A1.** Real CLI QA, 2026-10-14, HEAD `bce2c992`, checkout `bea 0.3.1`, macOS 26.5.1, engine Python 3.12.
`bql-recipes.md` says "`cost(position)` reduces a position to its cost
currency — the right default for USD answers". That holds only for
postings held at `{cost}`. A foreign card charge such as `Expenses:Travel
300 EUR @ 1.10 USD` has a price but no cost, so `cost(position)` returns
300 EUR. The effects:

- Top expenses, burn and the close recurring grid report mixed EUR, JPY
  and USD inventories instead of the USD actually paid.
- The anomaly recipe (`number(cost(position)) > 200`) flags a 1,500 JPY
  lunch, which cost $10.50.

The skill warns only that `sum(position)` can mix units, which implies
`cost(position)` does not. No product fix implemented.

Create disposable `/absolute/books/main.bean`:

```beancount
option "operating_currency" "USD"
2026-01-01 open Liabilities:Card USD
2026-01-01 open Expenses:Travel
2026-06-01 * "HOTEL PARIS" "room"
  Expenses:Travel   300 EUR @ 1.10 USD
  Liabilities:Card  -330 USD
2026-06-03 * "TOKYO RAMEN" "lunch"
  Expenses:Travel   1500 JPY @ 0.0070 USD
  Liabilities:Card  -10.50 USD
```

Use isolated BEA_CONFIG_DIR/XDG_CACHE_HOME, BEA_NO_UPDATE_NOTIFIER=1, no
inherited BEA_FILE/BEA_TOKEN, and closed stdin. Run recipe 1 as written,
then with `weight`:

```sh
bea -f /absolute/books/main.bean query "SELECT account, sum(cost(position)) AS total WHERE account ~ '^Expenses:' GROUP BY account"
bea -f /absolute/books/main.bean query "SELECT account, sum(weight) AS total WHERE account ~ '^Expenses:' GROUP BY account"
```

Actual: the recipe gives `Expenses:Travel  300 EUR  1500 JPY`. The
hand-computed answer is 340.50 USD.

On a larger fixture (split, refund, an at-cost buy, prices, boundary
dates), the hunter found:

- recipe 4 (burn) gives `300 EUR 1500 JPY 40 USD` instead of 380.50 USD;
- recipe 6 (anomalies) returns both the hotel and the ramen instead of
  the hotel only;
- the close recurring grid shows `300 EUR` and `1500 JPY` rows.

Reproduced twice by the hunter and once by me.

Control: `sum(weight)` gives `340.5000 USD`. On an at-cost holding,
`sum(weight)` equals `sum(cost(position))` (4,612.50 USD both ways), so
the switch does not regress cost-basis answers.

Expected: the recipes are meant to give USD answers to spending
questions. A foreign charge with an `@` price is ordinary card data and
should count at the USD paid.

Cause: the guidance and recipes that use `cost(position)` where `weight`
is meant:

- `skills/.claude/skills/beancount-ask/references/bql-recipes.md:9` (the
  claim), with recipes at about `:15`, `:25`, `:43`, `:56`, `:64` and
  `:73`.
- `skills/.claude/skills/beancount-close/references/close-checklist.md:35`
  (the recurring grid).

The eval fixtures contain no `@` prices, so CI never sees the difference.

Fix:

- Use `sum(weight)` and `number(weight)` in the spending, burn, anomaly
  and recurring recipes.
- Reword line 9 to say that `cost(position)` converts only `{cost}` lots,
  and that `weight` also converts `@`/`@@` prices. Use `convert(…)` with
  prices for market value.
- Add an `@`-priced foreign expense to an ask or close eval fixture, and
  mark the recipes so CI runs them.
- No dependency change.

Blast radius: every `beancount-ask` spending answer and every
`beancount-close` recurring-gap check on a ledger with foreign-currency
card charges, which are common for travel and online purchases.

Dedupe: searched `.pm/**`, including `done/`, for `cost(position)`,
`weight`, and foreign together with recipe. Related notes:

- w1/139: coalesce grouping.
- w1/140: the close date bound.
- 356: the flag sweep.
- w3/366: `list` display of `@` prices.
- w4/m11/t005 and w4/m26/t006: `cost(position)` for at-cost verification
  only.

None covers price-annotated postings in the skill recipes.

Acceptance: on the fixture, the top-expenses recipe returns 340.50 USD,
and the anomaly recipe returns only the hotel. The at-cost net-worth
answer is unchanged. The recipes run in skills CI against a fixture with
an `@` price.
