# On a host with fewer than three cores, a small ledger's parse waits behind a large ledger's

**Minor · ledger · 30–45m · A1.** Measured during w0/m5's closeout (2026-10-18).

The rustledger worker pool sizes itself to `max(1, min(2, availableParallelism() - 1))` (`backend-cluster/ledger/src/foundation/rustledger/rustledger-worker-pool.ts`). A host with one or two cores gets one worker. Every parse, query, filter, source-slice lookup and directive count shares that worker, so a small ledger's read waits for a large one's task to finish.

Measured with a 33 MB ledger (495,431 directives) being counted (`directive-count` task, about 2.2 s):
- 2 workers: a one-line ledger's parse answered in 1 ms.
- 1 worker: it answered in 2,170 ms.

The request thread is not involved. `/healthz` answered throughout (w0/m5), so this is pool capacity, not event-loop blocking. Whether the hosted `starter` plan gets one worker depends on its CPU allotment, and whether Node's `availableParallelism()` sees the container's quota or the host's cores. Neither is in this repository.

**Fix (pick one, with numbers):**
- Size the pool from an explicit setting with a floor of 2. On one core, the OS then time-slices a small parse in beside a large one.
- Or give small tasks their own worker or a priority lane, keyed by file-map size.
- Either way, record the worker count at startup so the deployed value is visible.

**Dedupe:** w0/m5 moved directive counting into the pool and does not change its size.
