# One entry dated in an early year (e.g. 0050) makes the balance sheet 23,718 monthly points (3 MB), and the app stalls: Accounts and Transactions stay on skeletons

**Severity:** minor (rare input, but it bricks the ledger in the app). **Estimate:** 1–2 hours (bounding the series; client and/or backend). Owner: mobile, with a backend-v2/ledger option. A2: w4/m38 made the backend accept directive dates in years 0001–0099, and a typo or a placeholder like `0001-01-01 open …` (compare w5/889) is enough to trigger this. The app requests the balance sheet with a monthly series from the ledger's first date to today. For a year-0050 entry, each series has **23,718 points**, the response is **2.97 MB** and takes about 8 s. After that, the app stops loading other screens.

Environment: `main` at `e87a32d61`+ (Metro `--clear`), dev build `io.beancount.ios` 1.20261002.50 on iPhone 17e (iOS 27.0). QA account, synthetic disposable ledger `mcp-agent-eval` plus `0050-01-01 open Assets:Qa20261020Ancient USD`, `0050-01-01 open Expenses:Qa20261020Ancient USD` and a 1.00 USD entry on `0050-03-01`. `getLedgerErrors` was empty. Restored byte-for-byte afterwards.

## Repro (observed)

1. Cold-launch with the fixture present. Home renders from cache, then shows **"Showing cached numbers — they may be out of date"**.
2. Accounts → **skeleton rows for more than 3 minutes**, across two relaunches. Transactions → skeleton rows too.
3. Each Accounts query, run directly against the API, answers in 0.4 s. The stall is client-side.
4. The app's `BalanceSheet` query (`balanceSheet.graphql`, `conversion: at_value`) returns `netWorthData`, `assetsData` and `liabilitiesData` with **23,718 points each**, 2,966,040 bytes, in 8.0 s. (The first server call after the write took 33.6 s.)
5. Remove the fixture and relaunch: Accounts loads immediately.

## Likely cause (mechanism not fully isolated)

- The series length is unbounded: one point per month since the first directive. Home charts at most 1Y/ALL windows, but it receives everything.
- The Apollo cache is persisted with `maxSize: 1 MB` and `trigger: "write"` (`mobile/src/common/apollo/cache-persist.ts`). A multi-MB entry is re-serialized on every cache write and then purged. A plausible reason other screens never settle is that this starves the JS thread. Not instrumented: profiling the JS thread would confirm it.

## Fix

Bound what Home fetches. Request the series with the active window (`time` from the range pill, or the last N months plus one prior point for the change label) instead of all history. Alternatively, have the backend cap or downsample interval series. Keep the persisted cache under its cap by excluding oversized fields, or switch persistence to a debounced trigger. Add a guard test with a 0001-01-01 directive.

**Acceptance:** with the fixture present, Accounts and Transactions load normally, Home shows no "cached numbers" banner after the network settles, and the BalanceSheet payload for the default 6M view is bounded (for example under 50 KB).

## Dedupe

w21/043 (done) fixed the year *display* (its recheck was blocked by this stall). w4/m38 (done) made the backend accept these years. w5/889 covers the `0001-01-01` CLI overflow. w21/006 and w21/047 cover cache staleness, not size. Searched `.pm/**` for payload size, series length, early year and cache size with mobile. No item.

---

**Blocked 2026-10-21** — Confirmed at HEAD: Home's `useBalanceSheet(ledgerId)` and `useBalanceSheetBasis(ledgerId)` request every series with no `time`. Every range pill, ALL included, slices that one full-history monthly series on the client, so a year-0050 entry yields about 23,700 points in each of six series.

Bounding it is a design choice, not a local fix:
- **Fetch per pill:** pass `time` per range pill. That gives up the instant, offline-capable pill switching and cold start that the m34 fetch-policy audit pinned, and ALL still needs the whole span.
- **Server cap or downsample:** cap or downsample interval series in backend-v2/ledger, an API change that must keep REST, GraphQL and MCP in parity.

The note also says the stall mechanism (persisting a multi-MB cache on every write, against the 1 MiB cap) is not instrumented. Confirming it needs JS-thread profiling on the simulator, which this run could not do.

No partial work was shipped.

**Unblock:** (1) the owner picks the approach: per-pill fetching on Home, or a server-side cap or downsample with REST/GraphQL/MCP parity. (2) Someone with the simulator profiles the stall, so the fix targets the real cause, for example excluding oversized fields from the persisted cache. Who can clear it: the repository owner, plus a native QA run.
