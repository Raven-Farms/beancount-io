# Money flow doesn't pad a real currency held at cost: "+$100.5" under a "+$100.50" headline (w21/506 over-reach)

**Severity:** low. **Estimate:** 10 minutes. Owner: mobile. A3 (code-level at `main` `dbe9b0144`, proven under `tsx`): `select-transaction-detail.ts:114-116` turns padding off for every posting at cost (`heldAtCost`), including ordinary currency lots. In an EUR ledger, `Assets:Bank:USD 100.5 USD {0.92 EUR}` reads "+$100.5" in Money flow under a "+$100.50" headline. An EUR lot (`4.5 EUR {1.10 USD}`) reads "+4.5 EUR", which is the exact w21/445 symptom back again.

## Fix

Keep recorded digits at cost only for codes that aren't ISO currencies, or that the ledger declares as commodities. Pad real currencies even when held at cost.

**Acceptance:** those rows read "+$100.50" and "+4.50 EUR", and "+10 BND {80 USD}" still reads "+10 BND". Unit cases are added.

## Dedupe

Over-reach of w21/506 (done); see w21/445. Searched `.pm/**` for heldAtCost.

---

**Blocked 2026-10-22** — Still wanted, but the two outcomes the acceptance asks for cannot both be produced from the data a posting carries. "+10 BND {80 USD}" must keep reading as shares and "100.5 USD {0.92 EUR}" must read as money, yet `BND` (a fund ticker that is also the Brunei dollar) and `USD` are both ISO codes held at a cost; nothing on the posting tells a lot of a currency from a lot of a fund. w21/506 chose "held at a cost is a commodity" because of exactly that ambiguity, and w21/601 now relies on the same rule for the headline. Telling them apart needs the ledger's `commodity` declarations (or their metadata), which no read the app makes returns, or a heuristic list of major currencies that no ticker collides with.

**Unblock:** the owner chooses a source for it: a read of declared commodities (with a flag for currencies) across REST, GraphQL and MCP and a deploy, or an accepted heuristic (a fixed set of major currencies treated as money even at a cost). Then it is a mobile change in `select-transaction-detail.ts` and `formatAmount`. Who can clear it: the repository owner (read contract or heuristic).
