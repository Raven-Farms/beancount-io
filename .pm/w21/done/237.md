# Home's balance cards can read "+$0.00 (−0.00%)" or "−$12.00 (−0.00%)": the percentage's sign and colour come from the unrounded change

**Severity:** cosmetic. It needs a large balance with a small move, or sub-cent market-valued holdings. **Estimate:** 10 minutes. Owner: mobile. A3: w21/055 made the amount's sign follow the rounded value, but the percentage still prepends "+" from the raw `change >= 0`, and `changePct.toFixed(2)` can print "-0.00".

Code-level at `main` `68c1c39cc`. A reviewer ran the exact expression under Node:
- baseline 2,500,000 → 2,499,988 gives `-$12.00 (-0.00%)`.
- baseline 10234.567891 → 10234.565 gives `+$0.00 (-0.00%)`, drawn in the unfavourable colour.

## Root cause

`mobile/src/common/d3/interactive-line-chart.tsx:437-445`: `${changeAmount} (${change >= 0 ? "+" : ""}${changePct.toFixed(2)}%)`.

## Fix

Round the percentage first, normalise `-0` to `0`, and take its sign (and the colour) from the rounded value, consistent with the amount: `-$12.00 (0.00%)`, `+$0.00 (0.00%)`.

**Acceptance:** unit cases for the two inputs above read `-$12.00 (0.00%)` and `+$0.00 (0.00%)` in the neutral colour.

## Dedupe

w21/055 (float residue in the amount) and w4/053 and w4/036 (zero baseline) are related but don't cover the percentage's rounding. Searched `.pm/**` for "-0.00%", "(+0.00%)" and changePercent.

Fixed in mobile: the chart's change row takes its percentage from `formatChangePercent`: the value is rounded to two places first, a rounded -0 is normalised to 0, and the sign (and whether the change is "flat") follow the rounded value, as the amount's sign has since w21/055. The two inputs in the note now read `-$12.00 (0.00%)` and `+$0.00 (0.00%)`, and a change that rounds to 0.00% is drawn in the neutral colour (`black60`) instead of the favourable or unfavourable one. Larger moves are unchanged (`+12.35%`, `-3.20%`). Tests: `formatChangePercent` for both inputs in both directions, the signed and rounding cases, and a wiring check on the chart (rounded text, the neutral colour, no sign built from the raw change); they fail with the chart reverted. Deviation: the neutral colour applies when the percentage rounds to zero, not when only the amount does (a nonzero percentage keeps its favourable colour), and no simulator run.
