# A budget (Custom) entry's hash ignores its account and amount, so a stale delete removes a different or changed budget without warning

**Severity:** major (silent loss of the wrong budget entry). **Estimate:** 1–2 hours. Owner: ledger, with backend-v2 and mobile follow-through (REST, GraphQL and MCP share the identity). A3: `entryHash` for `custom` directives (`backend-cluster/ledger/src/foundation/rustledger/entry-hash.ts:293-303`) hashes only date and type ("best-effort … NOT byte-equal to beancount when values are present"). Same-date directives are told apart only by an occurrence suffix (`…:1`) that shifts when a neighbour is added or removed. Mobile's budget delete (`budget-screen/hooks/use-delete-budget-entry.ts:29-57`) looks the hash up with a network-only entry context and deletes with that fresh `sha256sum`. The checksum lock therefore always matches whatever entry the hash now points to, and w21/393's "changed elsewhere" path can't fire.

Reproduced on production at `main` `bcb3891e6` (the disposable ledger, restored to `f1a0b83c`):
- **Amount change.** `2026-10-01 custom "budget" Income:QaGig "monthly" 0.00 USD` and the same line at `0.50 USD` both have `entry_hash 29a009ac5a376113e84dc3f33ac26567`. On iPhone 17e (fresh Metro with w21/393's fix), I opened Budget ▸ Income:QaGig history and changed that entry to 0.50 through the API. Then I tapped the row's trash icon; the dialog said "Delete the monthly budget of 0.00 USD … effective October 1, 2026?", and I tapped Delete. The 0.50 entry was deleted (commit "Delete entry"), with no "changed elsewhere" message.
- **A different account's budget.** `2026-03-01 custom "budget" Expenses:QaA … 100.00` and `… Expenses:QaB … 200.00` hash to `a3595a92…` and `a3595a92…:1`. After QaA was removed through the API, `getLedgerEntryContext(a3595a92…)` returns QaB's slice and a valid `sha256sum`. A delete from a screen still showing QaA would remove QaB.

## Fix

Include the custom directive's values (account, amount, currency, and any other args) in the hash, stringified the way the journal serialises them. Byte-equality with beancount is already lost for Custom, so a value-aware hash costs nothing there. Alternatively, make deletes and edits send the checksum of the slice the screen displayed instead of fetching a fresh one. Apply it identically on REST, GraphQL and MCP, and check the dashboard's budget delete, which uses the same identity.

**Acceptance:** after either change above, the stale delete is refused as changed or removed (w21/393's message), and the other budget survives. Hash tests cover two same-date budgets and an amount change.

## Dedupe

Related: w21/393 (its refusal path never fires for this), w4/232 and w21/040 (same-date budgets applying in the wrong order), w21/250 (transaction stale deletes, where hashes do include content), w6/008 (canonical entry hashes for tools). Searched `.pm/**` for entry_hash together with custom, "best-effort" together with hash, and budget together with delete and stale.

Resolved at the source, in the ledger service. A custom directive's entry hash now folds in its values (each hashed by its wire type and text: the account, the interval string, the amount's number and currency, …) instead of the date and type alone, so two budgets dated the same day for different accounts or amounts have different identities, and a budget whose amount was changed elsewhere no longer resolves under its old hash. Mobile's budget delete (w21/393) looks the hash up with a fresh entry context, which now answers NOT_FOUND for the stale hash, so its "changed or removed" refusal fires and the other budget survives; the dashboard's budget delete, REST, GraphQL and MCP all share this identity, so they follow. Tests cover two same-day budgets of different accounts and of different amounts, stability, a valueless custom directive, and the id map (removing one same-day budget leaves the other's id unchanged); the first two fail against the old hash. Beancount byte-parity was already lost for Custom (documented in the module) and the golden parity tests cover only the other directive types. Deviations: every custom directive's hash changes once at deploy, so a hash held by a client or link before it is not found afterwards (an ordinary refetch re-lists them); the ledger service needs a deploy; I did not repeat the note's two production reproductions, and I chose the hash change over its alternative of sending the displayed checksum.

Production recheck (2026-10-22, after `806a698d1`): not deployed yet. Two same-day budgets for Expenses:QaA and Expenses:QaB (disposable ledger, restored) still hash to `a3595a92…` and `a3595a92…:1`. Recheck after the ledger service deploys, then repeat w21/393's native stale-delete (change an entry's amount elsewhere, then delete it in the app; it should now be refused).
