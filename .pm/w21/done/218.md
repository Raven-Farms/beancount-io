# Home's budget panel treats income targets as spending caps: it announces a beaten income target as "Below target" and ranks it as most at risk

**Severity:** minor-to-moderate. VoiceOver contradicts the Budget card, and the panel's three slots go to the wrong rows. **Estimate:** 30 minutes. Owner: mobile. A3: the panel's spoken status maps `favorable` to "Below target" / "Above target", which is only right for expense caps and can never say "On target". Rows are ranked by raw `progressPercent`, so an income target beaten at 150% outranks an expense at 97% of its limit, or an income target that is far behind.

Reproduced natively on the iPhone 17e simulator (iOS 27.0) at `main` `24a2d3f2c`, on the disposable QA ledger (restored byte-for-byte to `f1a0b83c`). The fixtures `; qa-20261020-budinc` and `-budq` added a monthly budget of `Income:QaBudInc -1000 USD` with 1,500 earned on 6 October, and a quarterly budget of `Expenses:QaBudQtr 900 USD` with 250 spent.
- **Home panel, VoiceOver value of the income row:** "$1,500.00 of $1,000.00, 150% used, **Below target**". The row is listed first. Rechecked after w21/076's fix (`main` `7a7b0be95`): "$1,500.00 of $677.42, 221% used, **Below target**".
- **Budget screen, the same budget:** Income:QaBudInc · Monthly · **Above target**, variance **+$822.58** in green.

Running the selector under Node also shows a 1,500 / 1,500 expense announced as "Below target" while the card says "On target". With five budgets, Salary (beaten), Fun (over) and Rent (on target) fill the panel, while Food at 96.7% and Freelance at 10% of its income target are hidden.

## Root cause

- `mobile/src/screens/home-screen/selectors/select-budget-panel.ts:115` (`budgetPanelRowAccessibilityValue`, unchanged by w21/076's fix) uses `t(row.favorable ? "budgetBelowTarget" : "budgetAboveTarget")`.
- `:92-95` (`selectBudgetPanelRows`) ranks by `progressPercent`; w21/076 only moved an overspent 0 budget to the top. The original spec (w3/m1/t009, "most at-risk first") and w3/123 ("preserve income/expense favorability") ask for risk, not utilization.

## Fix

- Carry `stats.status` into the row and speak `t(STATUS_LABEL_KEYS[status])` (`budget-screen/selectors/budget-labels.ts:56`), as the card does.
- Rank unfavorable rows first. Order expenses by utilization and income by shortfall (lowest progress first), with the account as the tie-break.

**Acceptance:**
- In the fixture above, the panel announces the income row as "Above target".
- A Node test with the five-budget set shows Fun, Food and Freelance (or the documented order).

## Dedupe

w21/076 (done) prorated Home's budget to today and ranked an overspent 0 budget first, and w21/165 the 12-budget cap; neither covers status wording or ranking. Searched `.pm/**` for budgetPanelRowAccessibilityValue, budgetBelowTarget, "income target", "income budget" and ranking.

Fixed in mobile (`select-budget-panel.ts`): a panel row now carries the card's variance `status` and whether it is an income target, and its VoiceOver value uses `STATUS_LABEL_KEYS` like the Budget card, so a beaten income target says "Above target", a spend exactly at its cap says "On target", and an income target behind says "Below target" (the old favorable-based "Below/Above target" is kept only as a fallback for a row with no status). Ranking is by risk, not utilization: a zero budget already spent past, then anything off its target the wrong way (bigger miss first, so an income target far behind outranks a small overspend), then spending closest to its cap, then income that met or beat its target, then a budget not yet in effect, with the account as the tie-break. Documented order for the note's five-budget set: Freelance, Fun, Rent, Food, Salary. Deviation: an expense exactly on target (Rent, 100%) still ranks above Food at 96.7%, so with a 3-slot panel Rent keeps its slot where the note hoped Food would; the order is the principled one above. Tests: four cases in `select-budget-panel.test.ts` (wording for income beaten/behind, on target, the five-budget ranking, zero budget first) plus the existing status tests updated for the new row fields; they fail with the change reverted. No simulator run.
